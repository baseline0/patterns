Queue-based load leveling trades latency for stability

Statement

Inserting a queue between producers and consumers smooths bursty demand and protects downstream services, at the cost of added latency, backlog, and operational complexity.

Invariant

The queue decouples arrival rate from processing rate: accepted work is durable until a consumer processes it, so overload becomes backlog rather than immediate failure.

Absorb bursts at the boundaryProducers append work items to a buffer instead of calling the downstream service synchronously; amortized-constant append keeps admission cheap under bursts.
Consumers process at a steady rateWorkers dequeue from the front at a controlled pace; constant-time front removal supports steady drain without re-scanning buffered work.enqueue
Backpressure becomes queue depthWhen arrivals exceed service rate, latency grows with depth and unbounded queues risk memory/time exhaustion; bounds, TTLs, or dead-lettering make the trade-off explicit.enqueue, drain
Reliability gained, simplicity lostThe design improves resilience and elasticity but introduces eventual processing, ordering/idempotency concerns, monitoring of lag, and extra infrastructure to operate.backlog

Complexity

Admission and dequeue are O(1) amortized per item; end-to-end latency includes queue wait time proportional to backlog and service time.
