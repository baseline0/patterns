## Abstract

Introduces Halfway Escape Optimization, a quantum-inspired metaheuristic that models tunnelling and entanglement effects to move candidates between search regions. The paper evaluates it on fourteen thirty-dimensional benchmark functions against particle swarm, genetic, artificial fish swarm, grey wolf, and quantum-behaved particle swarm optimizers. It then tries two constrained engineering designs and a rice-classification task, reporting competitive optimizer performance and stronger accuracy on the classification example.

## Concepts

- **Halfway Escape Optimization**: A quantum-inspired metaheuristic whose move rule lets candidates leave a local region before settling.
- **Tunnelling metaphor**: The quantum-inspired escape idea that lets a candidate cross a barrier instead of only climbing downhill.
- **Benchmark suite**: Fourteen general optimization functions evaluated at dimension thirty to compare search behaviour.
- **Established optimizers**: PSO, GA, AFSA, GWO, and QPSO, used as comparison points for the new method.
- **Constrained design problems**: Pressure-vessel and tubular-column design tasks used to test feasibility beyond synthetic functions.

## Claims

- Halfway Escape Optimization is reported competitive with five established optimizers on fourteen benchmark functions at dimension thirty. (Benchmark evaluation; reported-experimental)
- The paper reports feasible engineering designs and higher accuracy on the Osmancik-97 and Cammeo rice classification tasks. (Applications; reported-experimental)
